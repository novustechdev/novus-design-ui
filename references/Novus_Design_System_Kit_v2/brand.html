The Novus design system

One source of truth for how Novus looks and sounds — visual and verbal — so the website, documents, letters, slides, apps and social all read as one company: consistent, connected, intuitive, minimal, uncluttered.

Essence

The four words every choice traces back to:

Trusted Precise Inclusive Enduring

Thesis

Novus looks and sounds like the infrastructure it builds — calm, precise, dependable, trusted without fuss — expressed light-first for the page and the boardroom, with a dark mode tuned for the screens that run the rails.

Sensory anchor

A calm 24/7 operations floor — monitored, orderly, mission-critical; mastery without drama. Every design choice is tested against it. If a choice feels loud, decorative, or trend-driven, it fails the anchor.

Voice

Held to the same standard as the visuals. We explain; we don't sell. Short sentences, concrete nouns, proof before adjectives — the senior engineer explaining clearly across a desk.

PlainPreciseProven

Model & terminology LOCKED

Novus's commercial model is Service as Software — we provision the technology and run the operations. Always frame the offer this way. Never use "Software as a Service" or "SaaS" in any material.

Proof & partner branding LOCKED

Keep the spotlight on Novus. Clients may appear as proof points — logos live in logos/clients/. Connectivity exception (approved): the national payment rails, QR networks and card schemes Novus is connected to may be shown as a factual "Connected across the region" strip (logos in logos/schemes/) — interoperability, not promotion. Vendors / technology-partners are still buffered: describe the capability generically and attribute the outcome to Novus. Lead with impact — weave financial-inclusion / ESG proof using the Signal Green accent.

Say this

  • "It runs 24/7."
  • "Here's what changed."
  • "We've done this before — here's the proof."
  • "This will work at national scale."

Not this

  • "We elevate your experience."
  • "Seamless, cutting-edge solutions."
  • "Unlock innovation."
  • "Leverage synergies."

Banned words (with reason)

  • elevate — says nothing; show the outcome.
  • seamless — promises friction-free we can't guarantee.
  • cutting-edge — trend-claim without proof; name the capability.
  • unlock / leverage / synergy — filler jargon; use plain verbs.
  • revolutionary / game-changing / world-class / robust — unprovable hype; give the number.

UI copy library

  • Empty — "No opportunities yet. Add one to begin."
  • Loading — "Loading live data…"
  • Error — "Couldn't reach SharePoint. Check your connection and try again."
  • Success — "Saved."
  • Destructive — "Delete this opportunity? This can't be undone."

House rules: British/Singapore English (specialise, colour); sentence case for headings & buttons; dates as 19 June 2026; numerals for 10+ with separators (8,426); money as SGD 1,200; emphasis by weight. Country order (locked): Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Cambodia, Philippines, Sri Lanka, Botswana.

Internal application headers — the two-mark rule LOCKED

Scope first. This rule applies only to internal applications and tools — software our own people use to do their work. It does not apply to anything a client, partner, investor or the public sees. On the website, decks, proposals, brochures, posters and any client-facing surface, the Novus master lockup is always on the left and prominent, per §4b. If you are unsure which a surface is, it is external and the Novus mark leads.

Why the inversion. An internal tool has an internal audience. Everyone who opens it already works here and already knows the Novus brand — so brand recognition is not the job this header has to do, and brand prominence buys nothing from a colleague. What the header must do instead is make the tool's purpose unmistakable: which of our applications is this, and what is it for. That is why the app's own mark takes the prominent position, top left, and the Novus mark moves to the endorsement position on the right. The moment the audience is external, that reasoning reverses — recognition is the job — and so does the layout.

Inside an internal app: the app lockup sits far left, top of the page, as the primary identifier. The Novus master lockup sits far right as the endorsement. The two marks are never adjacent — side by side they compete and neither reads. Between them: the context strap, then the utility cluster, then a 1px divider, then the master lockup. Below 520px the divider drops and the master steps to 20px, but it never disappears.

Web patterns CURRENT BUILD

The finalized homepage is the grounded reference — applied/novus-website.html. Conventions locked there:

  • Sticky nav with scroll-spy active-link underline, a hamburger menu under ~860px, and a moon/sun theme toggle.
  • Two-column hero with a subtle, reduced-motion-safe "signal" motif (concentric rings, gentle pulse) on the right.
  • Stat bands carry no divider lines or borders — figures sit on spacing alone.
  • Alternating section backgrounds (LOCKED): every section alternates plain ↔ tinted band so no two adjacent sections share a ground — this is what visually defines the sections. Order: Who (plain) · Platform (band) · Solutions (plain) · Perspectives (band) · Proven impact (plain) · Why Novus (band) → footer. Hero is exempt (reads into the plain Who section); the inclusion stats inside Proven impact keep the Signal-Green accent on Proven's plain ground. Re-check the alternation whenever sections are added, removed, merged or reordered.
  • Regional footprint is a real coastline map with no box or frame, in-map city pins (Singapore = HQ hub) and Botswana as a lower-left in-frame label.
  • Hover = clickable (LOCKED affordance): there is exactly ONE hover treatment — lift (translateY(-4px)) + raised shadow + accent border, with a focus highlight — and it always signals "this box opens something". It applies to every clickable box: the four product cards, Talent & Managed, white-paper cards, industry tiles, the architecture layers and the novaai core, and leadership cards. Non-clickable boxes never react to hover — info tiles ("Why Novus"), Proven case cards, client/scheme logo tiles and the value-chain strip stay static, because a box that responds but does nothing is a false affordance.
  • Proven carries a client-logo wall plus a connectivity (rails / QR / card-scheme) strip: marks capped to one optical height, shown in original full colour on white tiles (legible in dark mode), in compact dense-column tiles to limit scroll. Both are non-clickable, so neither reacts to hover.
  • Primary CTA (LOCKED): the conversion button is always "Talk to us", styled btn--primary, everywhere — nav, in-section and the closing band; never "Contact us" or a secondary style for the same action. The closing CTA is merged into the top of the footer (.foot-cta > .cta-row: copy left, button right, hairline divider above the footer columns), stacking under 760px.
  • Market key-figures live inside Perspectives (no standalone "Opportunity" section). Proven + Impact are combined under one title, "Proven impact" (case studies + the dark inclusion-stats band as one unit). Talent & Managed services are a second tier inside Solutions.
  • Nav & footer (LOCKED): primary nav is exactly Platform · Solutions · Perspectives · Proven impact · Why Novus (+ the "Talk to us" button). The "Who we are" section stays on the page but is deliberately kept OUT of the nav to de-clutter. The footer "Explore" column mirrors the nav exactly — same items, labels and order — and is kept in sync whenever the nav changes.
  • Buttons: primary/secondary/ghost/danger with hover, active press (1px nudge), and focus-visible ring; size variants btn--lg (hero CTAs) and btn--sm. Links use accent-text, underline on hover, scoped away from buttons and nav. CTA/button groups use the .row wrapping-flex utility.
  • Leadership is behind a click-disclosure — collapsed by default under a "Leadership team" toggle (button with aria-expanded; .collapse grid-rows reveal). Use click/tap, never hover (touch + a11y); content is visibility:hidden while collapsed so it leaves the tab order.
  • Dark-mode cohesion: the four product cards render fully in two shades of light blue (pictograph + suffix + bullet markers = --blue-300, "nova" = --blue-200); footprint map pins/links brighten to --accent-text on the navy ground.
  • Dark-mode parity (LOCKED): dark styling must apply under BOTH the JS toggle ([data-theme="dark"]) and the OS setting (@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) on :root:not([data-theme])). Every attribute-based dark rule is mirrored into the media query — logo→white, two-shade-blue lockups, map pins, bullet markers — so a device simply set to dark (or the JS-less preview) still looks right. Never rely on the toggle alone.
  • Responsive: multi-column grids use auto-fit minmax(≤240px,1fr) so they reflow without overflow down to ~320px; structural two-column layouts (hero, who+map, services, footer, architecture) stack at 760px; nav collapses to a hamburger at 860px. Headings & section padding use clamp().
  • Mobile-first build (LOCKED): design the phone layout FIRST, then scale up — base CSS targets mobile and larger screens layer with min-width queries (never max-width overrides). Test at 375px baseline (not 1440), touch targets ≥44px, thumb-reach nav/drawers/sheets, type via clamp() tuned small-first, and no hover-only interactions (hover is a bonus; tap is the path). Core open/close of menus, disclosures and modals must not depend on JavaScript where a native mechanism exists — prefer <details>/<summary> accordions, a checkbox+<label> drawer toggle, and :target detail sheets, so the UI still works if JS fails (this fixed the iOS-Safari burger menu). Verify at 375/768/desktop, light + dark, JS on and off. When desktop-pretty and mobile-usable conflict, mobile wins.
  • Country order (locked): Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Cambodia, Philippines, Sri Lanka, Botswana.

Second-level / interaction patterns CURRENT BUILD

  • Bulleted lists (LOCKED): hanging indent — the "›" marker sits in a fixed slot and wrapped text aligns under the text, never under the marker (.bullets: li{display:flex;gap}, marker flex:0 0 auto). Apply to all bullet lists in every material.
  • Reveal-more cards (LOCKED): any box that opens a second layer (modal/form) uses the standard clickable treatment — stretched-link .card-trigger + the lift affordance — and carries NO inner "explore"/"request" button or cue; the card itself is the control. Products, Talent/Managed, white-paper cards, industries, architecture layers + novaai core, and leadership cards all follow this.
  • Sticky-nav anchor offset (LOCKED): the nav is sticky, so html carries scroll-padding-top:5rem (≈ nav height) — in-page menu jumps clear the header and never hide the target section's title.
  • Detail "click for more": opens an in-page modal built on the native <dialog> element (free focus-trap, Escape, inert background; backdrop + close button dismiss). Used for every clickable box — the four solution cards, Talent & Managed, white-paper cards, industry tiles, the architecture layers + novaai core, and leadership bios.
  • Clickable cards: the whole card is one accessible target via a stretched-link overlay button (.card-trigger over a position:relative card) — no separate "explore"/"request" button; keyboard-operable with a focus ring. The card lifts on hover (the single "clickable" affordance); non-interactive boxes never do.
  • Lead-capture forms: "Talk to us" and "Request a white paper" open dialog forms collecting name, title, organisation, work email, mobile (white-paper adds a paper selector) with required-field validation; a white paper is never auto-sent — the requester is captured first.
  • Leadership: roster is collapsed behind a disclosure; each card is clickable and opens a bio modal (~300 words). Headshots are round, on a white background with a thin grey ring (.bio-photo / card .avatar--photo), with a monogram fallback.

Office document deliverables LOCKED · 2026-07-06

  • Tokens first, always: every generated Word/PDF deliverable (reports, memos, evaluations) is styled from tokens.css, read fresh in the producing session — never from memory of the brand. No ad-hoc hex values anywhere.
  • Colour map: headings/accent --blue-500; deep headings & emphasis --blue-700 (--accent-text); body text --neutral-900; secondary/muted --neutral-600; table header fill --blue-500 with --text-on-accent; row/panel tints --blue-50 / --neutral-50; severity text --red-600 / --amber-700 / --green-700.
  • Font must be EMBEDDED: Carlito, with the TTFs from fonts/ttf/ embedded into every .docx. macOS ships neither Carlito nor Calibri, so an un-embedded file silently substitutes fonts on other machines — naming the font is not compliance, embedding is.
  • Logos & bullets: official logo assets from logos/ placed at size parity (never retyped); bullet lists use the hanging-indent › convention. Render and visually verify before delivery.
  • Restraint & blue budget (LOCKED · §13): emphasis comes from weight and hairlines, not solid fills. Blue appears only as section headings, section/table hairlines, small emphasis and links — never as large solid fills repeated down a multi-table document. At most one solid-blue block per page-view; prefer a tinted callout (--blue-50 fill + --blue-700 text + a --blue-500 left rule) over a solid blue bar. Saturated brand-blue ≤ ~10% of any page's ink.
  • Tables — tinted, not solid (LOCKED · §13): for any document with more than one table the solid --blue-500 header is replaced by a tinted header — --blue-50 (or --neutral-50) fill, --blue-700 bold text, a single 1pt --blue-500 hairline along the header top. Body rows separated by 0.5–0.75pt --neutral-200 hairlines only (no boxed grids); zebra optional and very light (--neutral-50 only, never --blue-50). Numerals right-aligned/tabular. A solid --blue-500 header is reserved for at most one hero/summary table.
  • Document type ramp (LOCKED · §13): cover title 26pt --blue-700 · subtitle 14pt --neutral-600 · eyebrow 9pt --blue-500 uppercase · section head (1.0) 16pt --blue-500 + blue hairline · sub-head (1.1) 12pt --blue-700 · body 10.5pt --neutral-900 · caption/footer/page number 8pt --neutral-500. Footer & page number are caption-size and never larger than body or the cover subtitle.
  • Proposal cover (LOCKED · §13): master colour logo top-left (size parity) · eyebrow · title --blue-700 · subtitle --neutral-600 · issuing entity + date · --neutral-200 hairline over the copyright line. Client/partner logos are allowed on a proposal cover per the partner-branding rule — a "Prepared for" lockup in original full colour on white, size-matched and unboxed. Run the §13.F restraint checklist on the rendered PDF before delivery.
  • Single tone + black body (LOCKED · §13): a heading is ONE colour (--blue-700) — never two blues in one heading; body and table row-labels are black (--neutral-900). Blue is reserved for headings, table headers and small highlights only.
  • Tables & headings hold together (LOCKED · §13): a table never splits across a page (rows kept together; the whole table moves to the next page if it doesn't fit), and a heading or mini-label is never orphaned from the block it introduces (keep-with-next).
  • Emphasis & figures (LOCKED · §13): no italics — emphasise with bold, a boxed note or a lifted info-card (tint + border + accent left-rule + bold lead). Embedded figures/mockups are framed (--neutral-300 hairline) and centred; logos are placed unboxed. Prefer a KPI card band (fee + key highlights) over a lone big-number box.
  • Carlito-native foundation (LOCKED · §13): set the document theme + docDefaults to Carlito, not just per-run overrides, and embed the TTFs. macOS caveat: Word for Mac ignores fonts embedded in a file, so the recipient must install Carlito — embedding alone is not enough.
  • Images never stretch (LOCKED · §13): a logo/image in a flex or grid layout must carry an explicit width (or align-self:flex-start) + object-fit:contain so the container can't distort it — the recurring stretched-logo cause.
  • Sample Brand Board (LOCKED · §13): a design/identity proposal may include a Sample Brand Board appendix — an illustrative concept in the client's proposed palette (not Novus's), always marked "Illustrative concept — not final artwork", subject to trademark clearance, stakeholder review and client sign-off; plates are framed, one per page; the surrounding document stays Carlito.

  • Explicit slide tokens: title --slide-h1 ≈46px · hero/section divider --slide-hero ≈72px · body --slide-body ≈24px (floor ~18px) · bullets --slide-bullet ≈22px · KPI number --slide-kpi ≈54px · eyebrow --slide-eyebrow ≈17px (pair with uppercase + --tracking-wide).
  • Authoring (LOCKED): one idea per slide. Because the type is large, shorten the copy or split a dense slide into two — never shrink type below the scale to force a fit. Keep card/KPI captions to one line on dense slides.

Deck layout & footer safe-area LOCKED · §10

  • Footer band: every content slide reserves a bottom footer band (the slide's padding-bottom); nothing in the body may enter it. The body is hard-clipped (overflow:hidden) at the safe line, so an over-full slide clips cleanly above the footer instead of bleeding over it (classes .nslide / .nslide__body / .nslide__foot).
  • Page numbers are baked at build time, never set by runtime JS — a static export / PDF (WeasyPrint) runs no scripts, so JS-set numbers vanish. A page-count check does NOT catch footer overlap; verify every slide visually.
  • Self-contained output (LOCKED): the shipped file is a snapshot — read tokens.css at build time, inline it, embed Carlito (woff2) and every image as base64, and never <link> an external stylesheet at runtime; pin the theme with <html data-theme="light">. Runtime linking is forbidden because Safari blocks a file:// page from loading a parent/sibling-dir stylesheet → the deck opens as a black screen. (Build-time dependency, runtime independence — rebuild to re-snapshot whenever tokens change.)
  • Print-engine quirks (WeasyPrint): grid/flex items stretch to the tallest sibling (inflating cards) and nested flex can render blank — for height-sensitive blocks use block flow (margins) or the .bullets component, and always verify the exported PDF, not just the browser.

Last-mile polish & QC LOCKED · §11

  • Punchline budget: a closing / highlighted line fits ONE line at the bold slide-body size — hard cap ~58 chars; if longer, cut it, never orphan 1–3 words on a 2nd line.
  • Annotation markers on a screenshot/mockup are subtle by default (~20px, translucent fill, coloured ring + numeral) and sit in the margin — never centred on text, a price, total or button.
  • Composed images (device showcase, charts) reuse the deck's footer safe-area + type scale — don't bake a separate footer with its own geometry.
  • Automated gate: slide-template/build/qc_check.py renders the HTML through WeasyPrint and fails loudly on any defect (punchline > 1 line, footer-band crossing, page/section mismatch, KPI wider than its cell, orphan tail). Still review at 2× / zoom for what code can't see.
  • Deck layout & typography (LOCKED, 2026-08-05 · §11.K): a hard footer safe-area — no element below the top of the nav bar (≥12px clear; every generator carries a SAFE_BOTTOM constant and checks each element against it); fill the box — content distributed through each slide's and card's vertical space, never top-sandwiched over a dead lower half (grow type + box heights together; box body ≥16px/12pt, tile headings ≥20px/15pt); hanging-indent bullets in every format — in PPTX native buChar bullets (U+203A, marker in the section accent via buClr), never a "› " text run; one line-spacing per deck (standard 1.2) on all multi-line text; the PDF of a PPTX deck is exported from the finished PPTX, never a parallel layout; and visual QA happens at full page size on the densest slides, never thumbnails only.

PowerPoint theme & template LOCKED · §12

  • One hand-maintained template: slide-template/pptx/Novus_Template.potx — a slide master + 17 real layouts (Title → Section Divider → Agenda → content types → Closing → Blank). Edited directly in PowerPoint; the theme colours/fonts are a live snapshot of tokens.css (parsed by novus_pptx_theme.py).
  • Canvas / fonts: 16:9 at 13.333in × 7.5in (= 1280×720 at px/96), side margin 0.667in, footer hairline + baked "N / total" numbers. Major + minor font = Carlito (Office users must install it, free & metric-compatible with Calibri, so PPTX matches web/PDF).
  • Content style is a choice (LOCKED): per section, pick a list (bullets) or cards (boxes/highlights); plus KPI/stat and Two-Column utilities. A colour-coded section nav bar ("you are here") is baked into the footer.
  • Copy-paste brand slides: the .potx ships Brand Lockups (8 product/platform lockups + master logo) and Brand Logos (master + wordmark in all 6 treatments) as real selectable slides — copy logos in Normal view, no Slide-Master needed (delete before sending). Helper grid also at slide-template/pptx/Novus_Brand_Lockups.pptx.
  • Verticals always branded (LOCKED): any product / platform sub-brand mention inherits its tokens lockup (lowercase, nova = blue-400, suffix = its --accent-text) plus its pictograph when standalone — never plain typed text. In manual / Copilot decks, apply the lockup yourself (PowerPoint won't auto-format it).
  • Section colour — one scheme (LOCKED · §12): every content-type slide (list, cards, KPI, two-column) is section-aware — the eyebrow text, its leading dot and the lit footer nav segment all use the same bright section accent (e.g. green-500 #00A04A for Platform), never the darker −700 step, so title and navigation match in HTML and PPTX. Non-content slides (Title, Agenda, Divider, Closing) show no active segment.
  • Nav bar (LOCKED · updated 2026-07-07): one segment per section (count follows the deck), each carrying its own section colour — faded for the inactive (tints #B2D4EC #F8E3B2 #B2E2C9 #CBC9E9 #B2C7D7 #F8D7C1 #CBCFD4 #B6D4C3 #DCCFB3 #C2DCF0) and full-colour + thicker (a taller pill) for the active section — a clear “you are here” tracker, identical in PPTX and HTML; the tracker appears on CONTENT slides only — Title, Agenda, Divider and Closing carry no nav bar.
  • Title slide = one design (LOCKED · §D4): the PPTX Title is the same plate as the HTML title — white lockup top-left, kicker + headline + strapline left, the globe hero-map right. The globe is a frozen frame cropped from page 1 of the WeasyPrint PDF at 3 px per css px (never redrawn, never another graphic; the corp orbit plate and title_bg_white.png are superseded), keyed to transparency on the flat ground so it composites with no seam. The globe plots the 7-market footprint — Singapore·HQ, Kuala Lumpur, Jakarta, Phnom Penh, Manila, Colombo, Botswana.
  • Rendering parity — HTML is the source of truth (LOCKED · §D4 · 2026-08-18): a deck is one design in three renderings — HTML (master), PDF (WeasyPrint print of that HTML) and PPTX (for people who type into it). PDF and PPTX mimic the HTML: same plate, same geometry, same type scale, same chrome; they may only be static where the HTML animates. Geometry converts through one constant — 1 css px = 1/96 in = 0.75 pt (46px title = 34.5pt, 24px body = 18pt, 22px bullet = 16.5pt, 17px caption = 12.75pt), with line-height set as exact point leading, never a multiple. The eyebrow + dot, title, nav bar, footer rule, logo, strap and “n / N” are drawn by the generator on a blank layout, not inherited from the .potx — which is why the section count can be data (4–10) and why Novus_Template.potx is never regenerated. Where template and HTML disagree, the HTML wins.
  • Platform architecture block (LOCKED · §D3): present the platform building blocks as three modular-component lockups (novahub · novaware · novaserve) with novaai as a full-width band spanning all three, carrying its lockup + “AI Intelligence Core · Data Flywheel” and three pips (pulsing in HTML, static in PPTX) — never a plain bullet list. App interface conventions

Rules for Novus apps and web UIs (added 2026-06-25). Enforced in tokens.css; applied across the novaRADAR / Revenue Management apps.

  • White ground. The page background is always --bg (white in light) — never --bg-subtle / #F4F7FA, which reads as a faint green tint beside white cards.
  • Dark mode + toggle. Ship an explicit [data-theme] light/dark layer and a persisted moon/sun toggle; default to the OS (prefers-color-scheme); apply the saved choice before paint.
  • Logo swap. Colour lockup in light, white lockup in dark via .brandlogo--light / .brandlogo--dark; never set inline display on the logo images (it defeats the swap).
  • Dark-mode text. Never put hardcoded dark text on a token surface; use --accent-text / --success-text / --danger-text. Fixed light-tint chips are fine.
  • Elevation on white. Cards rest on --shadow-2 and rise to --shadow-3 on hover; hover-lift only on clickable cards.
  • Colour discipline. Blue (--accent) is the accent; green is success/status only, never a ground; one accent per view.
  • Traffic-light status. Metric boxes carry status colour: neutral for descriptive/actual data (targets, booked actuals); green for a positive / on-track trend or forecast; amber for at-risk; red for alert. Left accent on metric cards, top accent + tinted header on grouped/quarter cards, with a one-line legend. Never colour a pure-data box.
  • Weighted categorical pickers. When a picker’s options carry an inherent weight / probability / score (e.g. sales stages), order them by weight in progression order (never alphabetical) and show the weight inline as a % (e.g. Proposal (60%)). Keep the option value as the bare key (save / calc unaffected); mirror the order + % wherever the category is displayed.
  • CW labels. Always show the week-start date next to a calendar week — CWnn (D Mon) (e.g. CW27 (29 Jun)) — everywhere a CW appears (badges, tables, pickers, history).
  • Risk / alert messages. Any textual risk / alert / warning message is shown in red (--danger) — red text or a red box — distinct from the traffic-light metric colours.
  • Self-describing chips & Intelligence Score. Metric chips prefix the field name — e.g. Stage · Lead · 30%, Intelligence Score · 15% · Upside. Opportunity confidence uses the transparent stage-based Intelligence Score, not subjective probability.
  • Token-driven styling. Every design value (colour, spacing, radius, type, shadow) comes from a tokens.css variable or a component class — never ad-hoc literals. Apps inline the full token set (incl. --radius-*).
  • Dashboard charts (anti-Excel). Style charts, don't ship stock ones: token font, rounded bars (top segment only), soft dashed gridlines, no axis border/ticks, dark rounded tooltips, constrained bar thickness. A primary time-series shows composition (stacked bars) and trajectory (a line across period totals). Charts must reflect real data — bucket revenue by its actual billing/close month, never smear an annual figure evenly. Flag anomalies with a hollow-stick marker: a dashed average line + on any period beyond ±18% of the mean an outlined point + capped stick + outlined pill with signed % vs avg (▲ up-spike, ▼ drop).
  • Back navigation. A single global ◀ Back must return to the previous state from any screen and never dead-end. Don't rely on raw history.back() (it ignores modals). Drive an explicit in-app view stack: all navigation flows through one _go() (push current view, close any open modal, apply the new one); goBack() closes an open modal first, else pops the stack; mirror browser/hardware back into it.
  • Insight / signal cards (verdict over chart). For leadership views whose goal is a judgement, lead with plain-language signal cards instead of a chart to decode: an uppercase eyebrow (signal type), a headline finding with the number emphasised (e.g. "Sri Lanka = 52% of pipeline"), a sub-line stating the risk, a traffic-light left accent by severity, and an optional small support visual (mini bars / chips). Most-severe first; open with a one-line verdict. Prefer this whenever a chart would only be re-read into a sentence.
  • Portable build. Single-file apps inline tokens.css, embed Carlito, and use the component classes.
  • App shell. Header and nav are full-bleed bars, but their inner content aligns to the same centered column as main — every app shares one content width, --container = 1200px (padding --gutter) — use .appbar/.appbar__inner and .appnav/.appnav__inner. The logo/menu never float to the window edge.
  • Logo size. The Novus master lockup is the same official asset in every app at --logo-height (26px) — one step smaller than the app name, which stays the primary identifier. Colour→white via .brandlogo--*; no per-app responsive shrink. The header lockup is always the official asset — never overridden by a per-app uploaded logo.
  • Responsive / multi-device LOCKED — every app is built mobile-first and stays fully usable on phones, tablets and desktop: no horizontal scroll, no clipped or overlapping content, tap targets ≥44px, readable type at any width. Always ship the viewport meta; use fluid grids (auto-fit/auto-fill minmax()), clamp() type/spacing, and breakpoints that stack multi-column layouts to one column on phones, wrap toolbars and make dialogs near-full-width. Verify at ~390px / ~768px / desktop before shipping. Universal, not optional.
  • Stat / KPI no-overlap LOCKED — big numbers must never collide: .stat__value is white-space:nowrap and .stat is min-width:0; lay figures out with .statrow (auto-fit grid, min column ≥ widest number) so they reflow to fewer columns instead of touching, with an explicit column-gap. Shrink the value font on compact cards. Never a fixed row that forces numbers together.
  • Source-file integrity LOCKED — any app that surfaces files stored under Novus 2nd Brain exposes them read-only (open/view only, never edit in place) to protect the masters, and always offers a separate Download (download attribute) so users work on a copy. Never wire a control that writes back to the original; state the policy in the UI. On file:// open links same-tab (browsers block target=_blank to local files); use _blank only when served.
