Usage

  • Sign-in sits on the kit's near-white ground (--bg-subtle) like every other screen, with the card lifted off it by a border and a soft shadow. The deep brand panel the kit used earlier is gone.
  • The split is 50/50 from 900px and stacks below it, brand panel first.
  • The brand panel carries ambient line art: slow loops drawn from tokens in light tints, the one decoration the kit allows, and it stops under prefers-reduced-motion. It never shifts layout or takes pointer events.
  • The lockup keeps its normal light-mode treatment here, since the panel is a light surface; no dark-lockup swap is needed.
  • Single sign-on sits under a labelled divider as a secondary action, so the password form keeps the one primary button.
  • No theme toggle on this page: the theme belongs in the user menu after sign-in, and portals render light by default.
  • The brand panel carries the product lockup in the dark-lockup treatment, one line of promise, and at most five capability chips. Below 900px it stacks above the card.
  • The title names what is being signed in to, followed by one line of sub-copy. Labels are small caps with a red required marker; inputs are filled so they read as places to type.
  • One full-width primary action. The reset link sits centred under it, and the Novus endorsement signs the card off.
  • Wrong credentials show one inline error under the username and keep what was typed. Never clear the form.
  • The password toggle is a real button named "Show password" or "Hide password"; the console layer hides it when scripting is unavailable, so a page never shows a dead control.

Usage — forgot password

  • Forgot password is its own screen, not a disclosure on the sign-in card. The link and every step's back link are the same affordance: .authcard__back, full width, centred, accent, underlined.
  • Three screens: request (username only) → link sent → choose a new password. The brand panel drops away from step one — once someone has decided they are locked out, the artwork is in the way.
  • Step two never confirms an account exists. "If an account exists for that username" is the whole message, so the screen cannot be used to enumerate users; it names the inbox and the expiry, not the person.
  • Step three pairs the two fields and their autocomplete tokens (new-password, not current-password), so a password manager offers to generate one instead of filling the old.
  • A bad or expired link is an .alert--danger at the top of step three with the request form still usable underneath — never a dead end that sends the user back to the start.
  • Password policy is one line of sub-copy. Do not build a live rules checklist: the server is the authority and a client-side list always disagrees with it eventually.

Usage — single sign-on

  • Where SSO is one option among several it sits under the or divider as a secondary button, and the password form keeps the view's one primary.
  • Where the workspace is SSO-only, delete the password form rather than disabling it, and promote the provider button with .authcard__sso--primary. A greyed-out password box reads as a broken login.
  • A failed SSO round trip is an .alert--danger naming what happened in the provider's terms, plus a retry that stays primary. Always keep the way back to password sign-in underneath.
  • Redirects are a loading state, not a screen: put data-loading="true" on the button that was pressed, or the user clicks twice and opens two tabs.
  • The provider name goes in the button ("Continue with Okta"), never a generic SSO icon alone. The user must know where they are being sent before they leave.
